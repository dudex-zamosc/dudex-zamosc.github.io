Varnishing and oiling is the final — and most important — step in hardwood floor restoration. The right finish not only enhances the natural beauty of the wood but also protects it against mechanical damage, moisture, and everyday wear. At Dudex, we use only premium quality products to guarantee a durable and beautiful result.

Choosing the right finish depends on the type of wood, the room's purpose, and your personal aesthetic preferences. Our specialists will help you select the ideal solution for your floor.

Types of Floor Finishes

Polyurethane Varnish

A traditional and proven choice offering the highest mechanical resistance.

Pros: High durability, excellent abrasion resistance, easy to maintain, available in matte, semi-matte, and glossy finish.

Cons: Longer drying time, slight odor during application, harder to spot-repair.

Best for: High-traffic areas, hallways, commercial spaces.

Water-Based Varnish

A modern and eco-friendly alternative, increasingly popular among our clients.

Pros: Fast drying, no unpleasant odor, eco-friendly, doesn't yellow over time, preserves the natural wood color.

Cons: Slightly lower mechanical resistance than polyurethane, higher price point.

Best for: Bedrooms, children's rooms, homes with allergy sufferers.

Floor Oil

A natural finish that penetrates the wood structure and highlights its character.

Pros: Natural, matte appearance, easy spot-repair, breathes with the wood, highlights grain structure.

Cons: Requires regular maintenance (every 1–2 years), lower stain resistance, longer absorption time.

Best for: Living rooms, rustic and natural interiors, exotic wood species.

Oil-Wax

A combination of oil and wax, merging the benefits of both products.

Pros: Natural look with added wax protection, easy application, good water resistance, spot-repair possible.

Cons: Requires periodic maintenance, slightly higher cost, needs dedicated cleaning products.

Best for: Living rooms, kitchens, modern interiors, oak floors.

Our Varnishing Process

1

Consultation and Product Selection

Together with you, we choose the type of finish, sheen level, and tone. We consider the wood species, room purpose, and your aesthetic preferences.

2

Surface Preparation

After sanding, we thoroughly vacuum the surface. We ensure the wood is clean, dry, and ready for finishing.

3

Priming

We apply a primer coat that ensures better varnish adhesion and evens out the wood's absorption. This is a crucial step for finish durability.

4

Applying Finish Coats

We apply 2–3 coats of varnish or oil, with inter-coat sanding between each layer. Every coat is applied evenly for a uniform result.

5

Quality Inspection and Handover

After the final coat has dried, we perform a quality inspection. We provide care instructions and guidance for the first days of use.

Frequently Asked Questions

Varnish or oil — which should I choose? +

The choice depends on your preferences and how the floor will be used. Varnish provides greater resistance and easier maintenance, while oil gives a more natural, matte look and allows for easy spot-repairs. We'll be happy to recommend the best option during our on-site inspection.

When can I use the floor after varnishing? +

After the final coat, you should wait at least 24 hours before light use (walking in socks). Full curing takes approximately 7–14 days — during this time, we recommend avoiding heavy furniture and rugs on the floor.

How many coats of varnish are applied? +

We typically apply one primer coat and 2–3 coats of the finish varnish. Each coat is sanded between applications to ensure excellent adhesion and a smooth finish. For high-traffic areas, we can add an extra protective layer.

How should I care for a varnished floor? +

Regular vacuuming and mopping with a slightly damp mop is essential. We recommend using dedicated cleaning products for varnished floors. It's also wise to use felt pads under furniture legs and avoid walking in hard-soled shoes. With proper care, your floor will maintain its beautiful appearance for many years.
